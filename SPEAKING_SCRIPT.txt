ANALOG ATLAS — approximately two minutes
Moon Base Rocket Science — Gerald Adli (solo)

SLIDE 1: Project and challenge
I’m Gerald Adli, the solo member of Moon Base Rocket Science. Our project, Analog Atlas, addresses the challenge of finding Earth analog locations for future Moon and Mars bases. It helps mission teams identify places to investigate before testing exploration robots.

SLIDE 2: Our method: OSEMN
We use OSEMN to collect and clean open data, screen 384 geographically spread Earth patches, rank terrain resemblance and interpret the evidence. A fixed random seed makes the sample repeatable with unchanged inputs. This is a pilot search with a weighted formula, not a trained AI model or a global census.

SLIDE 3: Demo: a candidate and its evidence
Here is an actual output from the project. A user runs the notebook, inspects a candidate on the map, then opens its evidence report. The cyan square marks Area A. The workflow connects the shortlist to repeat observations, quality exclusions and a concrete list of field checks.

SLIDE 4: Area A: repeat mineral signals
Area A shows hematite and clay signatures on nine qualifying dates. That supports investigating how rovers choose sampling locations. We retained fourteen of sixteen observation files. One excluded file missed the study area. Another had too few pixels passing quality masks. These are data exclusions, not a verdict on the site.

SLIDE 5: Terrain and limits
The median slope is about five degrees, but small rocks and traction remain unresolved. Field tests must confirm minerals and route suitability. Regional geology already has research, and exact-site novelty remains unproven. Earth cannot reproduce planetary gravity or radiation.

SLIDE 6: Next steps
Next, we need a local geology partner and a rover team. We would confirm permission, survey the route and verify mineral predictions. If suitable, a short rover trial would measure slip, navigation error and energy use. The goal is better evidence for choosing Earth rehearsals that support future planetary exploration.

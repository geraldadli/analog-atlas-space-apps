ANALOG ATLAS - JAKARTA
Approx. two minutes. SI-assisted script: OpenAI Codex.

SLIDE 1
Hello, I am Gerald Adli from Moon Base Rocket Science. Analog Atlas helps us find places on Earth where we can investigate and rehearse parts of future Moon and Mars missions. Our goal is to guide field tests with evidence, rather than claim an Earth site reproduces another world.

SLIDE 2
We use OSEMN: obtain, scrub, explore, model and interpret. NASA lunar and Mars terrain provides reference patches. We compare them with 384 sampled Earth patches, exclude listed analog sites and rank terrain resemblance. A fixed seed makes the sample repeatable. Mineral, temperature and snow checks come afterward.

SLIDE 3
Here is an actual output for Area A in Ha’il, Saudi Arabia. A user runs the notebook, inspects a candidate and reads the evidence before planning fieldwork. The cyan square marks a 200-metre inspection area. This image helps us locate it, but cannot show every obstacle a rover could encounter.

SLIDE 4
Area A has repeated hematite and clay spectral signatures on all nine qualifying dates. Across the wider area, fourteen of sixteen files passed the scene screen. One excluded file missed our boundary. The other had too few clear pixels. These are data exclusions, not evidence that Area A is unsuitable.

SLIDE 5
The terrain check gives a median slope near five degrees, but averages can hide dangerous rocks or ledges. We still need access permission, a detailed route survey and mineral confirmation. Regional geological research already exists, so we do not claim a newly discovered analog. Earth also cannot reproduce planetary gravity, atmosphere or radiation.

SLIDE 6
Our next step is a small, measured field trial with a local geology partner and a rover team. First, confirm access and minerals. Then survey a route before testing the rover. Analog Atlas helps choose where to investigate and what to measure, supporting preparation for future Moon and Mars missions.
